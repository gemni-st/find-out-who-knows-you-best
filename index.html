The newlywed game show format works even better with friends than with couples. Gather your group, hand everyone a whiteboard or a stack of cards, and prepare to learn exactly who has been paying attention all these years.

How to Play

One person is in the hot seat. Everyone else writes down what they think the hot seat's answer will be to each question. The hot seat reveals their answer, and anyone who matched earns a point. Rotate the hot seat until everyone has had a turn. Highest total wins.

Questions That Work Best

Pick questions with definite answers and a little personality. What is my most used emoji? What would I grab first in a fire? What is my unpopular opinion about a popular movie? What did I want to be when I was ten? The sweet spot is a question where close friends will nail it and casual friends will guess wildly.

Rules That Keep It Fair

  • No shouting out answers before cards are shown
  • The hot seat answers honestly, no tricking the group
  • Keep questions kind, nothing designed to embarrass anyone
  • Cap it at ten questions per person so energy stays high

The Reveal Is the Best Part

The scoring is secondary. The real entertainment is the debate after each reveal, when someone defends a wildly wrong guess with total confidence. To find out who knows you best, you need those moments of surprise, because they turn into the stories your group retells for months.

A Perfect Party Opener

This game needs no setup beyond pens and paper, which makes it ideal for the first thirty minutes of any gathering. By the time everyone has taken the hot seat, strangers feel like friends and old friends feel like family.
